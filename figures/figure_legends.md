All three were calibration targets, so this panel shows the fit, not an independent validation.

**Figure 2. The real wiring delays death in both connectomes.** (a, b) Fraction blocked vs time for the real wiring (3 noise seeds), degree-preserving rewired brains (5 rewirings) and the E→I control (excitation-to-inhibition targeting kept, E–I reciprocity broken; 2 rewirings), female (a) and male (b); each curve rises in steps as each optic lobe fails (Section 3.3). (c, d) t50 per run; bars are means. (e, f) First SD in brain neuropil (cubes labelled with a ventral-nerve-cord nerve, unassigned and unnamed cubes excluded; in the real male brains some of these fail about 5 s before the brain). Real vs rewired t50: complete separation in both sexes, one-sided Mann-Whitney p = 0.018 (3 vs 5).

**Figure 3. Where the brain dies.** (a, b) SD arrival maps for the real (a) and rewired (b) female brain on the same colour scale. (c) Arrival time of every 10 µm cube, rewired vs real (Spearman ρ = 0.94): rewiring makes every cube fail earlier, but the spatial order is largely kept. (d) Median arrival per brain region (left and right pooled).

**Figure 4. Which feature of the wiring protects.** Female: (a) t50 and (b) healthy firing rate (2 s before O₂ off) for the real wiring, keep_loops (only the E–I loop connections real; 2 rewirings), the E→I control (E→I targeting kept, loops broken), E/I-input-preserving rewiring (each neuron keeps its numbers of excitatory and inhibitory inputs; partners rewired; 5 rewirings), the same kept only for inhibitory receivers (WsignI, purple) or only for excitatory receivers (WsignE, gold; 2 rewirings each), full degree-preserving rewiring and break_loops (only the loops rewired, E→I and I→E targets pooled). (c, d) t50 of every run in every condition, both sexes (filled female, open male; markers explained below the panels), against the healthy rate (c) and against the firing rate at −40 mV mean resting potential during anoxia (d). Spearman ρ over condition means (16 conditions per sex): healthy rate −0.25 (female) and −0.38 (male); firing at −40 mV −0.94 and −0.89. The male E→I control fires like a rewired brain when healthy but dies 1.1 s after the real brain. (e) Recovery at the real healthy rate: the share of the real-minus-rewired gap that each control keeps, compared with rewired brains slowed to the same rate (Section 2.9); one run per control and sex. Dashed lines mark 0.33 and 0.67, the thresholds of the reading rules. Keeping the loop connections between their own neurons protects as much when their partners are shuffled (keepShuf) as with the real pairs (keep_loops), and about twice as much as non-loop E–I connections, even when these are matched in strength (keepEIs). break_loops is not shown, because it could not be brought to the real healthy rate (H16).

**Figure 5. Activity and gain.** (a) Steady healthy firing rate vs tonic drive (20-s runs with oxygen). The real wiring responds 2.0 (female) and 2.5 (male) times less to the same change in drive. (b, c) t50 vs steady healthy rate. Filled = frozen drive. Open = drive changed so each wiring fires at the other's healthy rate (rewired drive ×0.5, real drive ×2). Slowing the rewired brains leaves 77–82% of the gap; speeding up the real brain leaves 47–50%. (d) Mean firing rate vs mean resting potential during anoxia (0.5-s bins, from O₂ off until neurons start to block), every run; solid female, dashed male. (e) Firing at −40 mV per run, including the receiver-specific rewirings (not drawn in d). At the same depolarization, rewired brains fire 1.7–1.8 times faster than real ones; the E→I control, the E/I-input-preserving rewired brains and WsignI fire close to the real brain, and WsignE lies in between.

**Figure 6. Robustness.** Real minus comparison t50 for every test: dot = mean gap; bar = range over all pairs of runs; dashed line = the pre-registered 2-s threshold. Other measures of death: t50 of central-brain neurons only, and the median time at which the 41 virtual electrodes first exceed 20 mM K⁺. Model and protocol: lamina neurons removed, oxygen cut after a 20-s warm-up, and a clearance cut of 0.35 instead of 0.2 (identical to the original runs, because the cut acts only on pumps that have already failed). Glial capacity: the glial K⁺ store halved (5 mM) or doubled (20 mM; H9). Female photoreceptors at weight 0 (H14). Glutamate: glutamatergic synapses given weight 0 and the healthy rate restored by lowering all synaptic weights (primary, H10) or by lowering the drive; grey: weight-only control with glutamate inhibitory at the same reduced weights (no threshold; the ratio of the two gaps, 1.11 and 1.06, shows that glutamate's sign does not carry the protection). Activity matched: see Fig. 5b, c. Last rows: real brain vs E/I-input-preserving rewired brains; the remaining gap is below the threshold in the female (orange dot) and above it in the male. The receiver-specific rows (purple: inhibitory receivers keep their E/I counts; gold: excitatory receivers; last four rows driven to the real healthy rate, H12) are descriptive and have no threshold.

**Figure 7. Recovery after 60 s of anoxia** (female, real wiring, N₂ shaded). (a) DC proxy (mean neuron membrane potential); half-recovery 13.0 s after air returns (fly 11.8–16.1 s, Spong et al. 2016). (b) K⁺ at 41 virtual electrodes. (c) Fraction blocked and mean energy. The pump restart time constant (20 s) was fitted to the Spong recovery, so recovery is a calibration target.
